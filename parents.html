For parents

This page is for you, not for your child to play through alone. It covers the fuller briefing behind module 11, “When it feels weird.” The playable version your child sees is deliberately gentle — no graphic detail, no scary content.

What module 11 is really about

Requests for photos or secrets, gifts from strangers, and bullying. The playable version teaches one response only: stop, screenshot, tell a parent. If your child tells you something happened, the first job is to make telling feel safe — help first, no blame, no taking the phone away as punishment for telling.

Warning signs to know yourself: flattery (“you’re so mature for your age”), gifts or game currency, “keep this our secret,” moving a conversation to another app, and asking for photos. Weeks of being nice can be part of the approach. A request for a photo is the line — stop, screenshot, tell.

Three things to set up first

  • Ask to Buy: turn it on in Family Sharing so any purchase or download needs your approval. Money or installing anything means asking Dad first, every time.
  • AI companions: check for AI “friend” apps on her phone. Agree that AI chatbots are tools, not friends, and that she can always check a strange message with you.
  • Family code word: agree on a word anyone calling in a hurry — even with a familiar voice — must say. AI can now fake voices and videos, so the code word is the check.

School vs personal

Keep school and personal separate: the school account is district-owned and goes away when she leaves; personal things live in her personal account on home devices. Many apps are 13+, and lying about her age removes protections.

How to debrief

After each module, ask the four questions from the game: Who wants this? What do they get? Can I undo it? Would I be OK showing Dad? Praise telling you about a mistake more than getting a quiz answer right — “Oops is OK” only works if telling really is safe.
